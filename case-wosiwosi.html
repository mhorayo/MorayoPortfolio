Overview

A corridor millions rely on, served by apps that rarely earn trust

Every month, people across the UK and Nigeria send money home — school fees, bills, family support — without knowing what fee they'll pay or when the money will land. Wosi Wosi set out to change that: a remittance app that makes cost and timing visible before you commit.

I joined as UI/UX designer on a cross-functional team — product, CTO, marketing, and compliance. Before drawing any screen, the first job was understanding how remittance works as a business, where regulatory lines sit, and what "fast" and "trustworthy" must mean for someone moving real money across a border.

Strategic decision

How strict should verification be?

One decision shaped everything that followed: how much to ask for before letting someone send money.

Option A

Verify everything upfront

Full documents, address proof and income details before any transfer. Lowest compliance risk, but highest drop-off.

Option B

Verify nothing upfront

Let anyone transact immediately, gate verification at withdrawal. Fastest onboarding, but real compliance exposure.

Final decision

A three-tier verification system

Light verification unlocks small transfers on day one. Fuller documentation unlocks higher limits later. Compliance scales with usage instead of front-loading everything at signup.

Reflection

Challenges, lessons, and what's next

Balancing speed vs. compliance

The three-tier KYC system was the key compromise — letting users transact early while scaling verification with usage.

Designing for two currencies

Showing both NGN and GBP balances without visual clutter required careful hierarchy decisions on the home screen.

Trust before transaction

Every screen before the first transfer needed to build confidence — plain language, visible progress, no surprises.

Next steps

Post-launch: add push notifications for transfer status, referral rewards for corridor growth, and scheduled recurring transfers.
