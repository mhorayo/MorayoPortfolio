## Before
- She joined the team alongside the senior product designer. There was an existing design.
- The major issue: it wasn't converting. The company complained people weren't using the product and there were few customers.
- So it was BOTH a design problem and a marketing problem.

## Research
- She did research first. (Ask me to write a brief, honest research section that fits the case; she asked for this.)
- Then she interviewed current users about how they were using the app.

## First iteration
- She designed the first prototype; it onboarded users in a way that worked, and led to some increase in conversion.

## New problems (what didn't work)
1. Customer support complaints: the onboarding KYC was long and COMPULSORY: upload documents, fill in address and other details. Tiresome, so people dropped off.
2. The dashboard couldn't fit large balances: big numbers wrapped onto the next line. Not great at all.

## The pushback story (shows personality + collaboration)
- In the earlier prototype the CTO wanted multiple (shrinking) cards on the dashboard, so users could see it isn't only GBP, ready for more currencies later.
- She compared and contrasted, and argued for one full card at a time. The CTO and some team members pushed back.
- She suggested usability testing and observing how users actually interact.
- When the long-balance issue came through customer support, the answer was obvious, and they changed the design.

## Second iteration (what works)
- Dashboard: one big card at a time showing the full number; tap pills to move to the next card/currency.
- Onboarding: users can SKIP KYC. The dashboard shows a "Complete KYC" card so they know they can do it later.
- Tiered KYC: tiers limit transactions per day; to send more money / raise the limit, users complete KYC and upload documents.
- Result: users finish onboarding, send money and move through the app → higher conversion, more time spent in the app, lower drop-off.

## Analytics (her ongoing habit)
- She brought in UXCam, then PostHog later, then MS Clarity; she runs monthly report reviews.
- Recent observation: BMP doesn't process transfers from 9pm to 5am, but a lot of users try to transact in that window.
  She raised it and discussed an order queue: collect the orders overnight and process them at exactly 5am, while being completely transparent with users.
