OwnIt

Creatives send their work first and hope the client pays. I’ve done it, and so have many creatives I know. OwnIt lets you show the work safely and get paid before you hand it over.

Role
Product designer: research, UX and UI
Team
A small startup team
Stage
Live web prototype, in internal usability testing
Market
Nigerian photographers, designers and filmmakers first
01 — Why we’re building this

“Send it, I’ll pay you later.”

Every creative I know has heard that sentence. You finish the shoot, the edit or the logo. The client wants to see it first. You send it on WhatsApp, and then you wait.

Sometimes they pay. Sometimes they ask for “one more change”, then go quiet. Sometimes your work turns up online with your watermark cropped out, and you never see the money.

I’ve been that creative. So have photographers, designers and filmmakers around me. We all had the same workaround: blurry previews, screenshots with big watermarks, half the files now and half later, and a lot of awkward chasing.

Nobody should have to choose between showing their work and protecting it.

This is my change. My solution.

02 — The problem

The client needs to see the work. The creative needs to be paid. Today, one of them has to go first.

Creatives in Nigeria deliver most of their work over WhatsApp and email. Once the files are sent, the creative has nothing left to hold.

03 — Research and design thinking

I started with the people I know, then drew the whole loop on paper.

I talked with creatives around me about how they share work and how they get paid, and wrote the white paper for OwnIt. The answer kept coming back to one moment: the handover. Everything before it is trust. Everything after it is chasing.

So I sketched the loop with the handover moved to the end. The client sees everything they need to say yes, for free. The full files only leave when the money does.

How might we

How might we let a client see everything without giving anything away, and get the creative paid before they deliver?

04 — What is? The first five seconds

Before anything else, OwnIt says who it’s for.

The intro plays once, the first time you open OwnIt. Small cards of creatives’ work fly towards you, the line cycles through who it’s for, and it lands on the promise: Your work. Your terms. It’s about four seconds, with a Skip button, because nobody opens a tool to watch a film.

I kept the motion flat and calm on purpose. My first version was heavier, and on Safari it broke the sign-in video behind it. The final one uses only movement and fade, and the sign-in video waits until the intro is gone.

05 — What is? Onboarding

Four short questions, and the client’s page builds itself.

Sign up with an email code, no password to forget. Then one question per screen: your name, what you create, your studio and brand colour, and where the money should go. On the studio step, a live preview shows exactly what your client will see as you type.

The payout step checks the account name with the bank before you save it, and says plainly that OwnIt never holds the money. You can skip it, but OwnIt reminds you before your first client pays, so no money gets stuck.

  1. Sign up with an email code, no password
  2. Your name, and what you create
  3. Your studio and colour, with a live preview
  4. Where the money goes, checked with the bank
06 — What if? Upload, then share on WhatsApp

Three steps from finished work to a link in the client’s WhatsApp.

Drop in the work. Photos, videos and PDFs become watermarked previews. PSDs, RAW files and ZIPs stay locked until paid, so the client can see they exist without opening them.

Then who it’s for, the price, and how long the link lives. Before you create the link, OwnIt shows the client’s price, the 1.5% OwnIt fee and what you’ll receive, so there are no surprises. Then one tap shares it on WhatsApp, where Nigerian creatives already talk to their clients.

  1. Add the work: previews are watermarked, source files stay locked
  2. Who it’s for, the price, and what you’ll receive after fees
  3. Share on WhatsApp, email, or copy the link
07 — What wows? What the client sees

The client opens the link in WhatsApp. No app, no account, no sign-in.

The page carries the creative’s studio name and colour, not ours. The work shows at full quality with a small OwnIt mark and the client’s own email in the corner. Not a tiled watermark across the work, just enough to make a leak traceable. The locked files are listed with their sizes, so the client knows exactly what they’re paying for.

One button: Pay. Checkout is Paystack, by card, bank transfer or USSD, and the money goes straight to the creative’s bank. The moment it clears, the delivery page unlocks the full files.

08 — The creative’s desk

Every project, its status, and the money, in one place.

On a laptop, creatives see all their projects by status: sent, viewed, paid, delivered, expired. Each project shows when the client opened the link, the fee, what you’ll receive, and a reminder button. Every new account starts empty, with one clear next step, because a first account full of sample projects was more confusing than helpful.

The small calls

Trust is mostly made of small decisions.

  1. Previews at full quality, not blurryA blurry preview makes the work look worse. A small traceable mark protects it without hiding it.
  2. “Traceable”, never “screenshot-proof”No web page can stop a screenshot. We say what’s true: every preview carries the client’s email and every view is logged.
  3. OwnIt never holds the moneyPaystack pays the creative’s bank directly. Saying so on the payout step and at checkout answers the first worry on both sides.
  4. Links that work inside WhatsAppWhatsApp’s in-app browser can drop part of a link, so the preview link is built to survive it. The link preview shows the OwnIt card, never the creative’s work.
  5. Links expire24 hours, 3 days, or a set time. A deadline gives the client a reason to decide, and stops old work from staying online.
  6. Show the fee before the linkClient pays, OwnIt fee, you receive. Creatives see their real number before they send anything.
09 — What works?

10 — Branding, next

The brand is part of the work. Most of it isn’t built yet.

Inside the app, the basics are set: the keyhole mark, the “ownit.” wordmark, a dark theme and one quiet crimson. Calm and editorial, not neon, so the creative’s work is always the loudest thing on screen. The brand outside the app hasn’t been prototyped yet. That’s future work.
