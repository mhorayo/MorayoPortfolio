Help2Pay

People in Nigeria hold crypto, but life is paid in naira. Help2Pay turns USDT into money in your bank, and puts bills, gift cards and shopping in the same app.

Role
UI/UX designer: research, UX, UI, brand
Scope
1,000+ mobile screens, web app, marketing site
Team
Founders, product and engineering, with regular stand-ups
When
May to August 2025, Nigeria, remote
01 — Why Help2Pay

Your money is in crypto. Your light bill is in naira.

More and more people get paid in USDT: freelancers, remote workers, traders, families abroad sending money home. But rent, school fees, airtime and electricity are paid in naira.

Getting from one to the other usually means a crypto exchange built for traders, a peer-to-peer trade with a stranger, then a second app for bills and a third for gift cards. Every step is another screen of jargon and another place a payment can go wrong.

Help2Pay’s goal was simple: make crypto to fiat feel like a normal bank transfer, and keep everyday payments in the same place.

One app. From crypto to everyday life.

02 — The problem

Crypto apps are built for traders. Everyday users just want to pay for things.

Help2Pay already had a lot to offer: sending to banks, swaps, bills, gift cards and a shop. The problem was getting people to understand it, find it, and trust it with real money.

03 — Research

I studied the apps people already use, and what they leave out.

I looked closely at Trust Wallet and Binance, and others in the same business. They’re excellent at what they’re built for. But neither is built around the moment that matters to an everyday user in Lagos: turning crypto into naira and paying for something.

How we worked

Design, test, meet, reiterate. Again.

I worked with the founders, product and engineering through stand-ups and many review meetings. Every major flow went through internal moderated usability testing: I sat with people, gave them a task like “buy a gift card” or “send USDT to a bank”, watched where they got stuck, and took it back to the team.

04 — What is? The dashboard people didn’t understand

A big balance, four buttons, and everything else hidden.

The first dashboard was built around the balance: send, receive, swap, approval, then a banner and recent transactions. In testing, many users didn’t know what else Help2Pay could do. They didn’t know they could shop, buy gift cards or pay bills, because none of it was on the home screen. An earlier version with a crypto price list had the same gap.

What testing showedShopping, gift cards and bills were real features with no door on the home screen. People only found them by accident, from a banner or the tab bar.

Paper sketches

On paper first: give every service a door on the home screen.

Before touching Figma again, I sketched the home screen with the team’s feedback beside it. Keep the balance and the four money actions. Then add a services grid, so shopping, airtime, bills and gift cards are one tap from home, and put the exchange rate where people can see it.

05 — What if? A home that shows everything

After testing, the dashboard brings the core features to the front.

The reiterated home keeps the balance and send, receive, swap and buy at the top. Under it, a services grid: shopping, airtime top-up, pay bills, gift cards, deals, exchange, referrals. Then today’s exchange rate, so people know what their dollar is worth before they do anything.

New users get a short guided tour on first open, starting with “verify your identity”, because nothing else works until that’s done.

06 — What if? Send money, before and after

Show what lands in the bank before anyone taps confirm.

The old send screen asked people to pick Bank or Crypto, type a USDT amount, then “choose fiat currency” and add a bank in separate steps. The naira amount and the bank came last, as if they were an afterthought.

The new flow puts the currency pair and the live rate at the top: USDT to NGN, you send this, they get that. Bank, account number and a note sit right under it, with “save as beneficiary” for next time. Then a full summary, every fee and the receiving bank, before the money moves.

  1. The pair and live rate first: you send, they get
  2. A full summary: every fee and the bank, before it moves
  3. Sent, with a clear end
07 — What wows? Gift cards, before and after

From a wall of logos to cards you can actually choose.

The first gift card screen was a carousel and a grid of logos, with no names, prices or deals. You could spot the brands, but not what you’d actually be buying. The reiterated screen names every card and shows the discount, and buying one is a single form: price, country, quantity, delivery method, recipient and a personal message.

10 — Design system and accessibility

One system, checked against WCAG, documented for the people who build it.

I built a proper Figma component library for Help2Pay: colour, type, buttons in every state, action tiles, inputs and cards. Every colour pair was checked against WCAG AA contrast before it went into a component. That check set a rule early: never white text on gold.

The handover

Nothing left for developers to guess.

Every screen went to engineering with annotations and notes: which component it uses, which state, which token, and what happens on error. The documented library sped up developer handoff by 30%.

1 2 3
  1. Currency pair. Component Select/Currency, two instances. Tapping either opens the currency sheet.
  2. Amount and rate. Input/Amount with the live rate above. You send and they get update together.
  3. Recipient. Input/Default with label, placeholder and error state. Bank uses Select/Bank. Body text Open Sans 16, teal labels.
11 — Brand

A brand that looks like money you can trust.

I made the full brand identity from scratch: the logo, colours, typography and iconography. Deep teal for trust, gold for value, and a mark built from blocks, like coins stacking up.

  • Logo and mark
  • Deep teal and gold palette

  • Iconography
  • Applied: merch, stationery, billboard
12 — What works?

Moderated testing changed three core screens: the dashboard now shows every service, send money shows what lands in the bank before you confirm, and gift cards are named, priced and easy to buy.
