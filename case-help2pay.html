Overview

Crypto made simple for people who don't speak crypto

Help2Pay needed to make cryptocurrency accessible to everyday users — freelancers receiving USDT payments, diaspora families sending money home, small businesses accepting digital payments. The existing flows were scattered, jargon-heavy, and leaked users at every step.

I joined as the sole product designer, responsible for the complete mobile app, web dashboard, marketing site, and brand identity — from research through production handoff.

The problem

One platform, too many pain points

Users were bouncing between multiple platforms to complete basic financial tasks. Crypto jargon confused non-technical users. Fees appeared only at confirmation. And there was no business dashboard — just a consumer flow trying to serve everyone.

Scattered workflows

Send, swap, shop, and pay bills required different apps and interfaces. Users lost context switching between platforms.

Crypto jargon barrier

"Transfer Funds" instead of "Send." Technical language alienated the 70% of users who weren't crypto-native.

Hidden fees

Real costs only revealed at the final confirmation screen. Users felt deceived, abandoning transactions at the last step.

Feature discovery

Key services — gift cards, bill payments, exchange rates — were buried deep in navigation. Users never found what they needed.

Key iteration

The dashboard that hid features vs. the one that surfaced them

The original dashboard prioritised visual cleanliness over usability — hiding gift cards, bill payments, exchange rates, and referrals behind nested menus. Analytics showed users couldn't find core features, leading to low engagement on everything beyond send/receive. The redesigned dashboard brought every service above the fold with clear iconography and labels.

Core flow iteration

Send money — from confusion to clarity

The original send flow crammed currency selection, bank details, and conversion math into a single dense screen. Usability testing revealed users paused at the fee calculation — they couldn't tell what they'd actually receive. The redesigned flow separates concerns: choose currency, see live conversion with fee upfront, confirm bank, review summary. Each step has one job.

Before — dense single screen

Currency, bank, amount, and fees all on one screen. Users paused at conversion math.

After — progressive steps

Fee and rate visible before commitment. Bank details confirmed separately. One decision per screen.
